# All-key scale reference

The Scales tab has two collections. **Unit scales** offers preparation associated
with the selected guided unit. **All-key reference** keeps other keys, forms and
ranges available when music or a teacher calls for them. Choose **Pattern and
range**, then **Key**, and open a comfortable hand setup. The reference is
optional; its 441 setups are not a second course or a completion checklist.

The regular course remains eight units and 80 twenty-minute visits.
[Integrated lessons](integrated-lessons.md) connect technical preparation to
those visits' existing music. A full scale is useful when the music needs its
crossing; a five-note shape or released chord movement may be a better match
elsewhere. For example, Mozart's opening uses A4–E5 with fixed fingers, while
Canon bars 3–4 need specific LH bass-to-chord travel.

## Available material

There are 441 setups: 72 major scales, 216 minor scales, 144 major/minor
arpeggios, three contrary-motion scales and six chromatic patterns. Separate
hand and range choices account for much of that count. With 196 course
exercises, the active inventory is 637.

| Work | Scope |
|---|---|
| Major scales | All twelve pitch classes, spelled in practical major keys, including G-flat with an explanation of its F-sharp equivalent. |
| Minor scales | All twelve tonics in natural, harmonic and classical melodic forms. |
| Hands and range | Right hand, left hand and hands together; one and two octaves. |
| Arpeggios | Root-position major/minor, with their own crossing and continuation fingers. |
| Contrary motion | Complete C/G scales in opposite directions, beyond the course's five-note comparison. |
| Chromatic work | Every semitone from C, using chromatic fingering. |

Classical melodic minor raises degrees six and seven on ascent and returns to
natural minor on descent. The key signature remains the natural-minor signature;
changed degrees have written accidentals. Two-octave fingerings distinguish the
middle-tonic continuation from the final endpoint. Registers keep the hands on
separate keys, and exercises explain released travel rather than requiring a
held wide stretch.

## Practice and saved reports

Choose a pattern for a specific purpose. Locate its starting notes and changed
letters, recall each hand's fingering, join a comfortable chunk, then extend the
amount connected before increasing tempo. The suggested tempo is a reference,
not a pass mark. The A-major standalone RH octave worksheet remains available;
the course retains LH/both-hands assignments and RH crossing instructions while
giving Mozart its narrower musical preparation.

Saving records passage, hands, tempo, checks and a next-step note, plus the
reported scope: chunk, ascent or complete ascent and return. A selected full
score does not establish that the learner played it all. Comparable delayed
before-playback reports remain separate from later repair; older reports do not
gain invented scope evidence.

Saved reference practice joins shared Progress and cumulative review. It may
supply a due check inside a new guided visit's existing three-minute review
allocation, but a standalone reference visit does not complete a guided block.
The representative scheduler keeps technical key/form/range/hand setups
independent, including separate reference identities. A return to one setup
does not credit another. The guided page shows representative skill checks
separately from raw passages available for return; optional exploration can add
to that backlog. Consolidation and ordinary lesson choices remain available
without a requirement to finish the reference bank.
The browser remembers collection, pattern and key in the current tab. Saved
history uses the normal durable profile and JSON backup; unfinished drafts use
the existing same-tab scope.

Other current-build tabs save through the same serialized profile protocol, so
ordinary reports do not replace each other's history. An ordinary remote update
keeps the open lesson and draft; import/reset deliberately replaces the profile
and invalidates stale work. Saved-profile writes require Web Locks. Unsupported
browsers show a warning and retain read/backup access instead of using an unsafe
write fallback. Older already-open app builds outside that protocol are not
covered. [Data guidance](../README.md#data-installation-and-compatibility) explains
the boundary.

## Verification and limits

Automated checks compare parsed notes with independently specified intervals,
minor-form descent, full ranges, hand registers, arpeggio tones, semitone steps
and actual contrary motion. Shared checks cover per-bar duration, clefs, piano
range and hand conflicts. Progress tests cover scope and separation from guided
completion. Current release evidence is recorded in
[release readiness](release-readiness.md).

The scores and teaching text are original Woodshed material. Fingering
references recorded in the modules include
[Eastern Illinois University's charts](https://www.eiu.edu/music/student_resources/2204_Scales.Arpeggios.pdf),
[Margaret Denton's minor-scale charts](https://margaretdentonpiano.com/MInors%20natural%20harmonic%20melodic.pdf),
[the University of Miami reference](https://www.cs.miami.edu/home/burt/music/finger-charts.html)
and [John Wiles's chromatic chart](https://www.swindonpianolessons.co.uk/general/scale-fingering-chromatic/).
Independent teacher review of physical comfort and the learner pilot remain
pending; this inventory does not establish an exam grade or teaching efficacy.
