# Teacher review and learner pilot

**Status: PENDING.** This is a usable review protocol and blank record, not
completed teacher feedback or evidence of learning. It complements the
[software release checklist](release-readiness.md).

The goal is to determine whether a returning adult can understand, perform and
resume the assigned small tasks comfortably, and whether saved notes support a
later return. This small pilot can expose problems; it cannot establish efficacy,
an exam level, optimal spacing or a validated mastery threshold.

## Teacher spot-check across all eight units

Use a teacher familiar with adult piano learning. Record candidate commit,
reviewer, date, instrument and chosen comfortable tempos. Read each unit guide,
inspect the assigned preparation and returning B-session goal, then perform the
sample from its score before hearing the model. Compare the score, sounded
pitches/rhythm, fingering and instructions. A static notation test cannot judge
physical comfort, musical usefulness or whether a transition teaches enough.

Minimum sample: **three lessons per unit (24 total)** — one preparation, one
coordination/harmony task and one musical destination. Also inspect all three
placement prompts, one before-playback review and one eligible fresh example.
Open any source disclosure named below. This is a spot-check; it does not certify
all 196 course exercises, 441 optional reference setups or every source edition.

| Unit | Preparation | Coordination/harmony | Musical destination and specific concern |
|---|---|---|---|

Check that this LH repair precedes melody. |

The optional [all-key reference](continuing-scales.md) needs an additional technical
sample: B-flat and E-flat starts; B-major LH; a black-tonic major; the middle-tonic
continuation of a two-octave scale; D/E/G minor; a minor form whose melodic ascent
and natural descent change fingering; one major and one minor arpeggio; a complete
contrary-motion scale; and chromatic fingering. Check both hands against the
written registers and ask the learner to identify a comfortable chunk before
connecting the full range. This sample extends the review; it has not been
performed by an independent teacher.

Use the [integration map](integrated-lessons.md) to examine preparation followed
by application in the same visit. Check all three new preparations above. Ask
whether their actual registers, fingering and movements make the named passage
easier to understand and attempt. In A major, confirm that the full RH octave
worksheet can remain optional while scheduled LH/both-hands work still teaches
crossing; do not insert that crossing into Mozart's fixed five-note opening.
The twenty-minute budgets remain repeatable structures, not evidence that the
material's pacing suits a learner without revision.

Check the new written/listening feedback alongside the Canon, Mozart and blues
preparations. The bank now has 50 active questions, 15 historical definitions,
and nine supported fresh-example families (200 finite studies). Same-day concept
correction must remain pending for a later-date correct answer; zero-minute
answer reports must not count as piano practice or complete a block. Fresh
examples for the three preparations must retain the learned register, movement,
fixed fingers or chord-tone line while changing the order. A teacher should
judge whether that change tests useful transfer rather than another memorized
exercise.

Observe review burden as well as correctness. The interface distinguishes
recommended skill checks from the raw passage backlog; a family representative
must not be mistaken for a successful check of its siblings. A difficult passage
keeps its own repair. When offered, a consolidation visit can take one of the
normal practice slots; regular lessons remain available and the plan may require
more days. At most one new consolidation is recommended per day after a saved
check, while an unfinished visit stays resumable. The synthetic all-ready model
still leaves overdue work, so record actual burden, assistance and deferrals
rather than assuming every review clock is serviced.

For every sample, rate the following independently as **Accept / Revise /
Cannot judge**, with a concrete bar, sentence or interaction. An Accept is the
reviewer's judgment for this task and learner profile, not a proficiency grade.

| Dimension | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| Musical correctness | Do score, counting, note names, octave/register, harmony, fingering and sounded timing agree? Are pickup/partial bars and rests unambiguous? |
| Preparation | Are all new reading, rhythm, harmony and coordination demands introduced before combination? Can the teacher name an unexplained prerequisite? |
| Physical comfort | Can the stated movement be made without a fixed strain? Is a smaller passage, released movement or simpler alternative explicit? |
| Goal and feedback | Is there one observable musical criterion? Can a learner identify the bar/detail that needs repair rather than merely clicking Ready? |
| Returning project | Do successive B visits develop the same musical project with clear passage/goal changes and a useful next-step note? |
| Listening and expression | Does the text distinguish note/time synthesis from expressive performance, listening judgment and optional pedal? |
| First attempt and transfer | Is “before playback” clear? Does the variant change only the intended skill demands in familiar territory? Is repair kept separate from the first result? |
| Disclosure | Is sourced material identified accurately as an excerpt/arrangement, with the source and editorial limits visible? |

Finding severity: **Blocker** = incorrect music, unsafe/strained required movement,
unrecoverable data or a learner unable to continue; **Major** = misleading goal,
missing prerequisite or repeatable confusing interaction; **Minor** = local copy
or presentation friction. Resolve Blockers and Majors affecting the core before
sign-off. Record an explicit scope decision for an optional-tool finding.

## Seven-day learner pilot, with a Day-10 return

Recruit a small practical sample of **3–5 returning adult learners** with access
to a piano/keyboard; at least one should be unfamiliar with Woodshed. Record
prior playing/reading experience and device/browser. Let placement and the
teacher choose a comfortable starting unit; do not force all eight units into
one week. Teachers cover breadth, learners cover realistic use and return.

Use a disposable pilot profile or an exported backup. Explain what is recorded
and obtain permission before retaining any audio or sharing notes. Screen/audio
capture is optional. A normal learner profile must contain actual attempts,
minutes and dates; never backdate logs or manufacture readiness to make review
or transfer appear.

The scheduler's intervals are **gaps since a qualifying return**, not ages since
initial practice. A ready baseline on Day 0 is due around Day 3. If that return
qualifies, its next seven-day gap is due around Day 10. Therefore a seven-day
usability pilot alone cannot observe both gaps. Continue through Day 10 for that
follow-up, or explicitly leave the seven-day interval unobserved. Difficulty
changes the schedule; follow the real queue and record the changed plan.

| Time | Learner task | What to observe |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Placement, choose a starting unit, complete one planned visit. Pick a comfortable eligible anchor such as `rhythm-long-short` or `coord-held-bass`, practise it and save an honest report/next step. | Time to first meaningful task, understood goal, actual scope/tempo, comfort, ability to save/find next visit. Ready baseline is conditional on the learner's report. |
| Days 1–2 | Follow the next small session at a comfortable pace. Leave a block unfinished, navigate to Progress and resume; reload once. | Draft restoration, correct block, shared tempo, note usefulness and any lost/confusing state. Do not rehearse the anchor just to fill a pilot schedule. |
| Day 3, when actually due | Open the anchor's return. Try the same passage/hands/tempo once with the written score before app playback. Report, then listen/repair and save. | Did first-attempt instructions make sense? Record actual gap, first result, assistance, final result and one exact musical difference. Confirm 3+17 allocation if integrated. |
| Days 4–6 | Continue normal selected work. Include one Chords/Scales visit and one returning musical project. Skip or simplify when appropriate. | Cross-section credit, manageable burden, repeated musical value and whether the next-step note changes the work. Record rather than conceal difficulty. |
| Day 7 | Usability check-in, export backup, review history and pick the next musical goal. | Can the learner explain progress and the next action? Record 20-minute plan versus actual time and any help needed. This is not a seven-day retention gap after Day 3. |
| Around Day 10 | Perform the anchor's next return if its Day-3 result earned a seven-day interval. Same scope before playback, then comparison/repair. | Actual seven-day gap, comparable conditions and honest first/final reports. If eligibility/due date changed, record why; do not rename another interval. |
| When naturally offered | After two qualifying returns, open a supported fresh example before hearing it and save the first/final reports. This may require a later due visit after Day 10. | Identify one detail transferred and one repair; verify familiar key/register and distinct example. Otherwise mark fresh-example learner evidence PENDING, not failed. |

Also check a resized window, an exact later-block draft and an ordinary update
from another current-build tab: the open lesson/take should remain in place.
Use exported/disposable data for import/reset conflict tests. Those deliberate
replacements invalidate stale practice; they must not restore old history. The
saved-profile protocol requires Web Locks, and older builds that do not follow
it are outside its guarantee. [Audit fixes](audit-fixes.md) lists these software
scenarios separately from physical playing and learning evidence.

There is no required Ready count, speed gain or completion streak. Tiredness,
difficulty or a smaller comfortable passage is useful evidence. A smaller scope
must be recorded; it cannot count as a comparable full-scope return by assertion.

At the end of each visit ask:

1. What were you trying to improve, and what did you hear or feel that changed?
2. What would you work on next without someone explaining the screen?
3. Did any screen, control, instruction or movement make you stop or guess?

At Day 7/10 ask whether the learner would choose another visit, what musical
project they want to retain, and whether the review felt relevant. Record the
reason, not only a satisfaction number. Participation/return counts are
feasibility signals; a small convenience pilot cannot establish retention gains.
