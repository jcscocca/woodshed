# Topic coverage in our piano program

Bastien is used only as the user's overall lesson-topic benchmark. Woodshed retains its own key-based sequence, repertoire, teaching style, Chords/Scales/Guided Practice sections, shared progress and five days of two twenty-minute sessions. No page order or music is copied. The comparison uses the official Piano Basics Level 2 Piano/Theory/Technic contents; it does not claim coverage of the complete four-level series or every printed etude.

All 29 benchmark areas below have instruction, concrete application and learner-observable checks. The course schedules the preparation its music needs; optional technical worksheets remain available where an application uses a narrower shape. Inline conceptual questions provide additional feedback where appropriate. A correct written/listening answer does not establish performed piano competence, and a saved session records practice rather than mastery.

The active bank has 50 conceptual questions and 200 bounded transfer studies in
nine supported skill families. Fifteen historical questions remain readable in
saved reports. Four new questions cover the Canon placements, Mozart's fixed
position and blues seventh-chord colour. Answer-only repair records zero piano
minutes; same-day correction needs later-date confirmation. These counts show
available instruction, not validated learning coverage or examination readiness.

Sources: [Piano WP202](https://media.kjos.com/kjos/global.mt.lldns.net/kjos/pdf/WP202_TBL_S.pdf), [Theory WP207](https://media.kjos.com/kjos/global.mt.lldns.net/kjos/pdf/WP207_TBL_S.pdf), [Technic WP217](https://media.kjos.com/kjos/global.mt.lldns.net/kjos/pdf/WP217_TBL_S.pdf). All contents pages were visually examined.

## Completion checks

Automated coverage checks require referenced lessons to exist with practical guidance/checks, and the course's required topics to have scheduled instruction. The standalone A-major RH octave worksheet is optional; its crossing is still taught in the scheduled paired-hands work. Every listed concept check is attached to relevant instruction. Separate musical regressions verify actual notes, registers, source melodies, scale fingerings, chord spellings, complete forms, role changes and the performed repeat-ending route. D/A/E inversion passages each receive their own scheduled visit. Changed session plans use versioned identities; old saved block positions remain attached to the original plan.

An explicit exception matters in C Day 4 B: the original chord worksheet's
C–G–Am–F order becomes C–Am–F–G in *After the Rain*. The preparation's first three
LH bars match; its final G2–B2–C3 hold differs from the destination's four
G2–B2–D3–B2 quarters. The lesson names both changes and isolates the actual G
bar before adding melody. All 637 existing scores remain unchanged by the audit
fixes. [Audit fixes](audit-fixes.md) records the software and review boundaries.

The course has eight units, 196 exercises and 80 twenty-minute sessions. Days can repeat; those allocations do not assert that all topics are mastered in eight calendar weeks. Full written scores, reference playback and all required instruction are included. External catalogue music is optional.

The optional [all-key reference](continuing-scales.md) extends that guided
baseline through all twelve major and minor tonics, natural/harmonic/classical
melodic forms, one- and two-octave ranges, root-position arpeggios, complete
contrary-motion scales and chromatic work. Its 441 setups are selected by key,
pattern and range, without an additional guided completion path. Their notes,
fingerings, registers and returns have dedicated musical and progress regressions.

[Integrated lessons](integrated-lessons.md) map 23 revised visits to the existing
pieces. Three new preparations address the Canon's Bm/F♯m LH travel, Mozart's
A4–E5 position and Switchyard's E7/A7/B7 tones. The remaining links reuse existing
preparation. There are 637 active exercises, 50 active conceptual questions and
nine supported transfer families with 200 finite studies. Fifteen historical question definitions remain
recognized for saved answers without adding new course assignments.
